Here are five developments shaping Ottawa and the National Capital Region today, with context on why they matter.

Ottawa voters split on city’s direction, put homelessness first for downtown

Nearly half of Ottawa residents (48 per cent) hold a neutral view of the direction the city is heading, while 34 per cent say it is on the right track and 15 per cent say it is headed the wrong way, according to a Nanos Research survey for CTV News Ottawa. In a second release from the same poll, 48 per cent of residents said helping people experiencing homelessness should be the top priority for improving downtown, well ahead of public safety at 30 per cent. The survey of 762 Ottawa residents was conducted October 4 to 8 and is considered accurate within plus or minus 3.6 percentage points, 19 times out of 20. With the municipal election set for October 26, the large neutral bloc suggests many voters have not reached a firm verdict on City Hall. The downtown results also signal that candidates’ plans on homelessness and safety will weigh heavily on how voters judge the core’s future. Sources: Nanos Research, Nanos Research and CTV News Ottawa.

Former rivals Kenney and Notley unite as Alberta referendum enters final week

Former Alberta premiers Jason Kenney and Rachel Notley, whose parties faced off in 2019 when Kenney’s United Conservatives defeated Notley’s NDP government, are scheduled to speak together in Edmonton today at an event hosted by the Edmonton Chamber of Commerce, The Canadian Press reports. The pair are making the case for Alberta to stay in Canada ahead of the October 19 referendum, which asks 10 questions, the last on whether the province should remain in Canada or hold a second, binding vote on leaving. Advance voting stations open today. Sources: CP24.

Canada sends its largest trade mission to India in over 30 years

International Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu is leading nearly 300 Canadian business representatives on a trade mission to India from October 12 to 17, with stops including Mumbai and Bengaluru, according to Global Affairs Canada. The department calls it the largest Canadian trade delegation to India in more than 30 years. Sources: Global Affairs Canada.

France says it won’t issue crab licence off Newfoundland without talking to Canada

France’s new ambassador to Canada, Hélène Farnaud-Defromont, says France has not issued a snow crab fishing licence off Newfoundland and would not take that step without consulting Canada, The Canadian Press reports. French President Emmanuel Macron had reportedly promised an exploratory licence. Sources: iNFOnews.

Canada joins 28 countries condemning Iran after U.K. air base plot

Canada has signed a joint statement with 28 other countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, France and Germany, condemning Iran’s lethal plotting, arson, sabotage, cyber-attacks and other hostile activities against them and their partners, according to Global Affairs Canada. The statement backs the U.K.’s finding that Iran was involved in a September 27 incident at RAF Fairford. Sources: Global Affairs Canada.
